There are patterns in what you save. You just cannot see them yet.

That article about organizational design from January relates to something you saved last week about system architecture. Same underlying idea—coordination costs—but completely different domains. Those connections are where the aha moments live. But you would never find them on your own.

## The Problem with Saving Things

Everyone saves articles they never read. Browser bookmarks become digital graveyards. Read-later apps fill with hundreds of items marked "unread forever." The more you save, the more you lose.

The common assumption is that this is a discipline problem—if you just committed to reading more, you would extract value from your growing library. This assumption is wrong.

The real problem is not reading volume. It is **invisible connections**. No human can hold 200 articles in their head and find patterns across months of saved content. The value is not in any single article—it is in how ideas relate across your entire library.

## From Storage to Understanding

Most tools for saving articles focus on making saving easy, or on making reading pleasant. Both are useful, but they leave the hard part to you: noticing how the things you save relate to each other.

That is the part I wanted help with, so I built Resona around it. Rather than another archive that grows silently, it processes what you save and shows you how ideas connect.

## The Resonance Metaphor

The name comes from **resonance**—the phenomenon where frequencies align and amplify each other. When you strike a tuning fork, nearby objects at the same frequency begin to vibrate. One signal amplifies another.

This is what happens with ideas that resonate. You save an article about why startups break at 20 people. Months later, you save something about database sharding. Different domains, same underlying pattern: systems that worked at small scale require different coordination mechanisms at larger scale.

Each article carries its own **frequency**—a core idea or theme. Alone, they fade into your archive. Together, they resonate, amplifying understanding.

This audio metaphor runs through the product:
- **Signal Extraction** removes the noise to find the core ideas
- **The Weekly Digest** surfaces harmonics—themes that appear across articles
- **Chat** is a tuning fork—strike a question and see what articles vibrate in response

## What Resona Does

**Save without organizing.** A browser extension captures any article in one click. No folders, no tags, no decision fatigue. Just save and move on.

**Extract the signal.** AI processes each article to extract the core ideas: a summary, key points, notable quotes, actionable items. You get the understanding without reading everything.

**Surface connections automatically.** Every week, a digest arrives showing patterns across your library. "These two articles from different months share a theme. Here is what they say together." This is the moment Resona is built for.

**Chat with your library.** Ask questions like "What have I saved about team scaling?" and get answers synthesized from your saved content, with sources. Your past reading becomes queryable.

## The Weekly Digest: Where the Value Lives

Most knowledge tools require you to actively engage—highlight, annotate, organize, review. Resona inverts this. The system works for you.

The weekly digest is the core of the product. Even if you never open the app, every Sunday you receive:
- **This week's saves** with one-line summaries
- **Connections detected** across your library—articles that share themes you would never have noticed
- **Worth revisiting**—content from weeks ago, resurfaced before you forget

The aha moment comes when Resona connects two articles you saved months apart in completely different contexts. An article about jazz improvisation and one about remote team coordination—both about synchronizing without visual cues. That connection changes how you think about both.
